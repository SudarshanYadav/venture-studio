## What it means (observed, then inferred)
**Observed**
- The commonest complaint (32 of 87 low-star reviews in this sample) is "no change". Bengaluru reviewers are 28 of 181 (15%), the largest single city in the sample.
- At least 5 reviewers measured with a **TDS meter** and concluded the product failed. Brands answer this on their own pages: WaterScience ("Shower filters do not reduce TDS"; "convert hard water salts into harmless forms... without changing the TDS"), Purifit ("No shower or tap filter lowers TDS").
- Breakage and fit (24 of 87) are the second complaint, mostly about the CLEO tap filter's plastic neck.
- Hair and skin are why people buy (27 of 82 positive reviews), and why they get angry when nothing changes.

**Inferred (Medium confidence; sample is sorted negative-first, so shares are not market shares)**
- There is real, unprompted demand for *proof*: buyers want a number that moves. A hardness test (not TDS) that visibly changes would answer the most common complaint. **But only if the product truly keeps the water soft for the whole shower and for months.** If it does not, a strip demo is the kind of thing these reviewers already call "cheating" and "fake".
- Renters and PG residents appear (quote 25, 17), confirming that a no-plumber fitting matters.
- Positive reviews are dominated by feelings (hair, skin, lather) that cannot be measured or claimed. A clustered run of polished 5-star reviews in Feb to Jul 2025 on F2 and F3 is visible; I make no claim about how they were obtained.

**Not yet known (needs Mode 3, founder OK to contact people)**
- How people with the problem who have *not* bought talk about it (Reddit and apartment WhatsApp groups would show this; blocked here).
- Willingness to pay for a product that actually softens (a bathroom-scale unit at ₹6k to 15k) versus a ₹1.5k to 2.8k "conditioner".

## What it means (observed, then inferred)
**Observed:** the "no change" complaint recurs on every category (cartridge, liquid, tank media); D'Cal's one- and two-star share (22%) matches the first-screen shower filters (17 to 23%). Hair and skin is again the dominant motive, now also on liquids and tank media. Buyers of real salt softeners (Kent) talk about regeneration and capacity, not "no change", but only two such reviews exist and they sit on the brand's own site.
**Inferred (Medium):** unprompted proof demand (TDS meters, boiling tests, hot-water tests) shows buyers want a visible number; a hardness test strip before and after is the right proof, not TDS. A softener buyer's second worry is operating effort (salt, regeneration), which the first screen did not capture.
**Not known:** how non-buyers with the problem talk (Reddit, WhatsApp groups); willingness to pay for ₹10k+.
